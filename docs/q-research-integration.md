## HMM and Bayesian basket increment — 12 September 2026

The workbench now exposes **Run HMM multi-strategy allocation** and **Optimize a cointegration basket** alongside the existing nine actions. Both use frozen price histories, typed configurations, the bounded shared queue and immutable exports. The original repository's 183 recorded files were rehashed with zero mismatches, and its Git checkout remains clean.

`qresearch/equity_account.py` separates whole-share accounting from signal generation. It freezes quantities at each observed close and permits fills only at the next close, with adverse slippage, commission, short collateral, gross limits and per-observation borrow. Rebalances are atomic across sleeves; unavailable funding rejects the entire request. Independent tests reconstruct cash, positions, reserves and every equity row, and reconcile sleeve attribution to NAV. This is an explicit research account model, not a broker settlement or jurisdictional margin implementation. Sharpe, Sortino, annualized return, drawdown and Calmar use the configured observation count; undefined ratios remain null.

`qresearch/regimes.py` combines the source moving-average momentum and mean-reversion strategies with EG/Johansen statistical arbitrage. Expanding HMM refits use only observations available at the decision close. Evaluation uses forward filtering, never backward smoothing or Viterbi decoding over future evaluation observations. Three filtered probabilities blend fixed strategy budgets, including lower total exposure in the high-volatility state. The static-budget comparison uses the same signals and account. Saved fit diagnostics include scaler parameters, transition matrices, emission parameters, likelihood histories, convergence status and cointegration evidence. State-conditional Sharpe, worst actual account drawdown in each state and strategy/state P&L are retained. A finite fit that does not reach likelihood tolerance is explicitly exploratory; the default 600-observation synthetic run has one such fit among six.

`qresearch/basket.py` fits initial cointegration weights on an inner calibration interval, then uses seeded sequential Optuna TPE to tune relative weights, lookback and entry/exit thresholds on the following validation account. It freezes that selection before refitting on the full outer training window and evaluating subsequent unseen observations. Baseline parameters are fixed at lookback 20, entry 2 and exit 0.5. Both policies have identical funding, dates and costs; each fold schedules liquidation and charges exit costs. Chained fold equity is labeled as independently funded experiments. Failed cointegration, failed refit gates or no eligible trial produce explicit cash policies. Full trials, fold boundaries, selected parameters, gates, signal diagnostics and account ledgers are exported.

The saved HMM allocation contains actual final marked net holdings. The basket allocation contains the final unexecuted signal, separately labeled from liquidated fold accounts. Both feed Q's risk simulation using the matching immutable history. The default synthetic basket run's final signal is cash, so its linked risk result correctly has zero portfolio risk. The default optimization did **not** improve the baseline: Sharpe difference was approximately -0.504, and the drawdown difference was approximately -0.827 percentage points. These are synthetic verification observations, not a claim about real trading performance.

A fresh-process replay exposed small HMM floating-point differences between server and CLI numerical thread counts. All registered quant computations and replay now use a single BLAS/OpenMP thread through `threadpoolctl`; that policy and dependency version are recorded in provenance. Tests replay the default HMM export in a subprocess with conflicting ambient thread settings. Historical exports retain their original fingerprints and source; newer code does not relabel them.

`qresearch/options.py` and `qresearch/cpp` adapt project 03 pricing and Greeks, with corrected deterministic forward moneyness, exact zero-time/volatility handling and explicit unavailable boundary Greeks. Vega/rho use absolute volatility/rate units; theta is annual. C++ builds locally with pinned `ziglang==0.13.0`, whose native C++ compiler is included in the shared requirements. Benchmarks preserve identical input rows, both sets of six outputs, compiler/flags/binary hash, all timing repetitions and per-Greek errors. Timing excludes compilation, process startup and I/O; language-specific result-container overhead remains included.

`option_simulation.py` reuses the source stochastic customer-flow generator and adapts its inventory quote skew. The separate `option_account.py` owns cash, collateral, fees and whole-unit positions. A quote and hedge target observed at event t become eligible at t+1. Hedge fills occur before new customer fills. Trades are all-or-none and may be rejected. Short-option full-notional reserves and 150% short-stock collateral are explicit research assumptions, without portfolio offsets or a claim to reproduce broker margin. Market-induced deficits are visible. The simulation ends strictly before expiry and marks open positions; no terminal liquidation, settlement, borrow, dividend or funding model is implied.

Hedging diagnostics show residual delta and the per-event mark change of positions held across the event boundary. The latter includes option time decay and excludes new fills and costs; it is not terminal payoff replication error. P&L attribution reconciles option and stock cash flows plus ending marks to total net P&L.

## Risk and modeling increment — 12 September 2026

`qresearch/history.py` freezes explicit multi-asset CSV imports, combines saved daily lab datasets, or generates a clearly labeled seeded common-trend scenario. Imported/source histories reject missing prices, mismatched dates or currencies, unsorted or duplicate dates and nonpositive/nonfinite values. The lab adapter uses UTC date labels and retains original source snapshots and their calendar/adjustment limitations. The UI exposes source-file attachment and saved-history selection. Histories are `quant_dataset` artifacts; source bytes and original records remain in their exports.

`risk_simulation.py` estimates historical log-return means and covariance and uses a Cholesky factor for 10,000–50,000 paths (bounded to 5,000,000 path steps). Four common-random-number scenarios cover baseline, volatility scaling, a blend toward positive correlation, and zero cross-correlation. Singular covariance adjustments are disclosed; constant assets retain zero variance. Each result contains every terminal portfolio loss/drawdown, pointwise percentile curves, per-asset price distribution summaries/histograms and the actual covariance/factor matrices. Signed static exposures and residual cash are projected; no future rebalancing, margin enforcement or liquidation is implied. VaR is a linear empirical loss quantile; expected shortfall integrates fractional tail mass.

`modeling.py` implements a constrained efficient frontier with dimensionally consistent diagonal shrinkage, explicit solver-success/constraint checks, and a saved minimum-variance allocation. Unlike the source notebook's full-sample weights presented as OOS, Q labels this frontier as in-sample estimation. That frozen allocation feeds risk directly, with exact history/date matching and retained source-result provenance.

ARIMA uses a fixed user-selected order, training-only scaling, past-only estimation, and state updates after each forecast. Both ARIMA and the naive last-observation baseline are evaluated on the same complete test window. Training ADF/ACF diagnostics and fit warnings are retained; constant or nonconverged fits do not masquerade as validated forecasts. Pointwise RMSE/MAE and forecast errors are saved. No stronger performance is guaranteed.
